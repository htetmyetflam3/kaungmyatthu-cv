The problem I solved

In a densely stocked automotive-parts shop, one shelf row can hold 30+ variants among 100+ pieces; similar-looking parts fit different vehicles, and other parts fit the same vehicle. A missing item gets assumed out of stock → replacements arrive → the original reappears → duplicates take scarce shelf space → the cycle repeats.

Look up the code in the shelf-location map.

STEP 2

Find the position

Main row → shelf → row in shelf (MR-2 / SH-1 / R3).

STEP 3

Confirm & replenish

Verify the variant, then replenish the same location.

The central question

Why can a clearly written prompt still produce an unexpected response?

I independently built a manuscript connecting practical prompting techniques with a personal “Layers & Zones” framework — context, premise acceptance, tone, inferred user knowledge and response constraints — instead of treating templates as the whole skill.

Self-authored manuscript LLM systems thinking Prompt engineering AI-assisted authoring Unfinished · personal exploration

Prompts as tasks

The conversation itself becomes the task. In LLM-based AI the user provides instructions, questions or examples and the system generates responses dynamically — in other AI types the task is embedded in control logic. Not all AI uses prompts; prompt-based interaction is what makes LLM-based AI fundamentally different.

Emergent behaviour, not magic

No single component decides. Just like a CPU, purposeful behaviour appears from the coordinated interaction of mathematical operations, learned parameters, sensory inputs and embedded rules — not an individual insight. Emergent behaviour results solely from the structured interaction of billions of parameters.

Layers & zones

Input layers decide whether a response is possible; adjustment layers decide how that response appears. Most “why did the AI respond like that?” moments trace back to this two-way split.

Read the manuscript — Layers & Zones + the full “Learn Prompting by Examples” chapter

Layers & zones — why a good prompt still fails

Throughout the manuscript, the word “layer” refers to two related but different functions. To understand why a good prompt can still produce unexpected results, separate:

  • Layers that evaluate the input — interpret intent and assumptions; evaluate context, constraints and risk; decide what is allowed, what is risky and what is relevant. Premise Layer → assumptions & intent · Safety Layer → policy & risk boundaries · Context Layer → scope, memory & framing. These layers do not write the answer — they decide whether an answer can be given, and under what conditions.
  • Layers that shape the output — modify tone, wording and structure; filter, soften, redirect or reframe content; apply constraints decided earlier by input layers. Safety Adjustment Layer → rewrites or limits output after safety approval · Tone Adjustment Layer → modulates style, formality & emotional distance · Knowledge Alignment Adjustment → selects an explanation strategy that fits the situation. They do not re-evaluate intent — they transform an already-approved response into a safer, clearer, more appropriate form.

Why it matters. A common confusion: “My prompt wasn’t unsafe — so why did the output feel restricted?” Because both things can be true at once — the Safety Layer approved the input, while the Safety Adjustment Layer still reshaped the output. In simple terms: if a layer judges the request, it operates on the input; if a layer reshapes the answer, it operates on the output.

Layers are conceptual models — they describe observable behaviour, not proprietary internal architecture.

Chapter: Learn Prompting by Examples — A Guide to Master Prompt Engineering

Even though prompt engineering is often talked about as a mysterious skill, at its core it’s about clarity, structure, and guidance. Knowing how to shape your instructions can drastically improve AI responses.

At a high level, there are several types of prompts—each serving a distinct purpose. We’ll go from foundational prompts to more complex, multi-step reasoning approaches.

1. Zero-Shot

Prompts — Clarity and Specificity

Zero-shot prompts rely on giving one clear instruction, without providing examples. Focus on clarity, specificity, and context to ensure the AI understands your goal.

Guidelines:

Use clear, unambiguous language

Specify exactly what you want (format, style, tone, output length, etc.)

Avoid vague or open-ended questions unless brainstorming is your goal

Examples:

Instead of: “Tell me about photosynthesis”Use: “Explain photosynthesis in simple terms suitable for a 10-year-old in under 100 words.”

Instead of: “How does a car engine work?”Use: “Explain the basic four-stroke cycle of a gasoline car engine so a high school student with no prior knowledge of mechanics can understand. Use an analogy to make it easier to grasp.”

Instead of: “Create a recipe”Use: “Create a simple recipe for a vegan chocolate chip cookie. Use common ingredients, prep time under 15 minutes, with step-by-step instructions.”

Instead of: “Give me some ideas for a blog post”Use: “Generate 5 blog post titles about the benefits of remote work for small businesses. The titles should be catchy and SEO-friendly, targeting an audience of entrepreneurs.”

2. Structured / Formatted Prompts

Structured prompts provide a framework that guides the AI clearly. They help make instructions precise and outputs predictable.

Intro

There are two main ways to structure prompts:

Format 1 – Sectioned / Structured Prompt

Task: Create a 3-day itinerary for a family visiting Paris.

Context: Include art, history, and food activities.

Output: Table with columns 'Day', 'Morning Activity', 'Afternoon Activity', 'Evening Activity'

Format 2 – Single-Paragraph / Condensed Prompt

Create a 3-day itinerary for a family visiting Paris. Include art, history, and food activities. Generate output as Table with columns 'Day', 'Morning Activity', 'Afternoon Activity', 'Evening Activity'.

Both formats functionally achieve the same output. The choice depends on readability, workflow preference, or AI compatibility. But for reusing purposes and to capture key elements in examples below, we will use Format 1.

Structured prompts often include:

Instruction / Task: What the AI should do

Context: Background info or references

Output format: JSON, table, list, bullet points, code, Excel, etc.

Example Skeleton

Task / Instruction: Summarize customer reviews into key points

Context: Reviews may include slang or abbreviations

Output format: Bullet points

Examples

Summarize customer reviews into key points based on the last 50 reviews, output as bullet points.

Create a 3-day itinerary for a family visiting Paris. Include art, history, and food activities. Output: Table with columns 'Day', 'Morning Activity', 'Afternoon Activity', 'Evening Activity'.

Write a Python function that returns the second-largest number in a list, handling duplicates and lists with fewer than two elements. Output: Python code with brief inline comments.

Write three tweets for an eco-friendly coffee cup launch. Output: Numbered list under 280 characters each, include #EcoFriendly.

Draft an interview invitation email for a Marketing Manager candidate. Output: Professional email template with placeholders [Candidate Name], date, and time.

Analyze customer feedback in CSV ('Feedback' and 'Rating'). Output: JSON object with keys 'positive_themes' and 'negative_themes', each listing top 3 recurring themes.

3. Role-Based / Persona Prompts

Ask AI to “act as” a specific role to influence tone, perspective, or style. This builds on structured prompts.

Key Elements:

Role / Persona: AI identity

Task / Instruction: What it should do in that role

Context / Background: Optional scenario

Output format: Optional

Examples:

Customer Reviews Summary

Role: “Act as a senior product analyst”

Prompt: "Summarize the last 50 customer reviews into key bullet points"

Follow-up: Highlight positive themes analytically, add top 3 negative points, provide a final recommendation paragraph

Paris Family Itinerary

Role: “Act as a professional travel guide”

Prompt: "Create a 3-day itinerary for a family visiting Paris"

Follow-up: Include art/history activities with insider tips, add recommended local restaurants, format as a table

Eco-Friendly Coffee Cup Tweets

Role: “Act as a social media manager targeting millennials”

Prompt: "Write three tweets for a new eco-friendly coffee cup launch"

Follow-up: Include #EcoFriendly, keep under 280 characters, make tone casual and engaging

4. Instruction + Constraint Prompts

Enforce rules like word count, tone, or format to match real-world needs.

Key Elements:

Task / Instruction

Constraints (word limit, tone, format)

Context / Background (optional)

Output format (optional)

Examples:

Customer Reviews Summary

Prompt: "Summarize 50 reviews into exactly 5 bullet points"

Constraint: Each bullet under 20 words, positive first, then negative

Follow-up: Ensure neutral, professional tone suitable for management presentation

Paris Family Itinerary

Prompt: "Create 3-day itinerary for Paris family"

Constraint: Each day has 3 activities under 4 hours

Follow-up: Format as table with 'Day', 'Morning', 'Afternoon', 'Evening'

Interview Invitation Email

Prompt: "Draft email for Marketing Manager candidate"

Constraint: Keep under 150 words, professional yet friendly

Follow-up: Include placeholders for name, date, and time

5. Iterative / Follow-Up Prompts

Refine outputs step by step without rewriting the original prompt.

Examples Using Skeletons:

Customer Reviews Summary

Initial Prompt: Summarize 50 reviews into bullet points

Follow-ups: Highlight positives, include top 3 negatives, add summary paragraph

Paris Itinerary

Initial: Create 3-day family itinerary

Follow-ups: Include art/history, add local restaurants, format in table

Python Function — Second Largest Number

Initial: Write Python function

Follow-ups: Handle empty lists, add inline comments, format clearly

6. Self-Improving / Feedback Prompts

AI can learn from its own outputs by giving it feedback iteratively.

Examples:

Customer Reviews Summary

Prompt: Summarize reviews

Feedback: "Make it more concise while keeping key themes"

Paris Itinerary

Prompt: Create 3-day itinerary

Feedback: "Adjust schedule to minimize travel and maximize sightseeing"

Eco-Friendly Coffee Tweets

Prompt: Draft tweets

Feedback: "Make tweets more humorous and engaging without exceeding 280 characters"

7. Comparative / Ranking Prompts

Compare or rank options based on criteria. Useful for decision-making or analysis.

Examples:

Customer Reviews Summary

Prompt: Rank top 5 positive themes by frequency

Follow-up: Explain reasoning

Paris Itinerary

Prompt: Rank top 3 activities for children

Follow-up: Explain choices based on convenience and fun

Eco-Friendly Coffee Tweets

Prompt: Rank tweets by engagement

Follow-up: Explain ranking reasoning including tone and hashtags

8. Chain-of-Thought / Step-by-Step Prompts

Guide AI to reason step by step, perfect for multi-step tasks or complex outputs.

Examples:

Customer Reviews Summary

Prompt: Summarize 50 reviews and explain reasoning

Follow-ups: List positives first with justification, then negatives, add summary paragraph

Python Function — Second Largest Number

Prompt: Write function and explain each step

Follow-ups: Describe handling of duplicates, behavior for <2 elements, format code with comments

Eco-Friendly Coffee Tweets

Prompt: Draft 3 tweets, explain why each appeals to millennials

Follow-ups: Highlight engaging words, suggest minor improvements for clarity or humor

9. Multi-Task / Combined Prompts

Have AI perform multiple tasks at once, keeping everything structured.

Examples:

Customer Reviews Summary

Prompt: Summarize reviews into key points and write recommendation paragraph

Follow-up: Highlight positive and negative themes, provide actionable advice

Python Function

Prompt: Write function and generate test cases

Follow-up: Explain logic step by step and expected outputs

Eco-Friendly Coffee Tweets

Prompt: Draft tweets and suggest posting schedule

Follow-up: Include hashtags, character count, and reasoning

10. Few-Shot Prompts

Expand a skeleton into a few-shot prompt with examples of input-output pairs to guide AI.

Key Elements:

Instruction / Task

Context / Background (optional)

Examples (input-output pairs)

Output format (optional)

Examples:

Extract company names from headlines

Input: "Apple announces new iPhone at their annual event." → Output: "Apple"

Input: "Shares of Microsoft surged after a positive earnings report." → Output: "Microsoft"

Input: "The new electric car from Tesla is getting great reviews." → Output: "Tesla"

Simplify complex sentences

Input: "The confluence of technological advancements… has culminated in a paradigm shift…" → Output: "New technology and changing customer tastes have transformed retail."

Categorize support tickets

Input: "I can't log in to my account." → Output: "Technical Issue"

Input: "How much does the premium plan cost?" → Output: "Billing Inquiry"

Input: "My order hasn't arrived yet." → Output: "Shipping Issue"

Convert natural language to SQL

Input: "Show me all users from Canada." → Output: SELECT * FROM users WHERE country = 'Canada';

Generate product descriptions

Input: "Product: Leather Wallet. Features: Hand-stitched, full-grain leather…" → Output: "Experience timeless craftsmanship with our hand-stitched leather wallet…"

11. Negative Prompts / Avoidance Instructions

Negative prompts guide AI by specifying what to avoid in its output.

Key Elements:

Task / Instruction

Avoid / Exclude: Specific words, tones, topics, or styles

Optional context / constraints

Examples:

Text — Blog Drafts

Task: Generate a 300-word blog post on sustainable travel

Avoid: Technical jargon, negative tone, clichés

Output: Friendly, concise, engaging paragraph

Image Generation — Landscape

Task: Create a scenic mountain landscape

Avoid: People, animals, text overlay, night scenes

Output: Bright daytime mountain view with realistic colors

Audio / Speech

Task: Generate a podcast intro for a health episode

Avoid: Background noise, informal slang, music overlay

Output: Clear, professional voiceover under 30 seconds

Data / Analysis

Task: Summarize customer feedback trends

Avoid: Speculation, unverifiable claims, personal opinions

Output: Objective bullet points with evidence from data

သူက ဖြစ်တည်မှုအားလုံးထက်ကျော်လွန်တဲ့အဆင့် ထိကို သင်ယူနိင်ခဲ့ပြီး ဖြစ်တည်မှုတွေရဲ့ မူလကနဦးအစကိုသိတဲ့သူ ဖြစ်လာခဲ့တယ်၊၊ အဲ့ဒါတောင် မှ သူ့ဖြစ်တည်မှုတန်ဖိုးသူသိပြီး သူ့ရဲ့လေးနက်တဲ့ ခံစားချက်တွေကိုပါဆုပ်ကိုင်ထားဖို့ရော ခက်ခဲတဲ့ မန္တာန်တွေအတွက်ရော သူ့စိတ် မှာနေရာ လွတ်တွေရှိသေးတယ်။ ဒီမန္တာန်တွေအာဂုံဆောင်ရွတ်ရတဲ့အလုပ်ကိုလဲ လေးလံကြာရှည်လွန်းတဲ့ ကမ္ဘာကြီးရဲ့အဆုံးသတ် နောက်ဆုံးနေဝင်ချိန်တွေ တစ်လျောက်လုံးမှ သူတစ်ကိုယ်တည်း တမြည့်မြည့်ရွတ် နေခဲ့တယ်။

အဲဒီတုန်းက...

သွားနေကြနေရာကို ပုံမှန်ခြေလှမ်းတွေနဲ့ ထုံစံအတိုင်း သွားရမယ့်လမ်းပေါ်မှာသွားနေတုန်း လူတစ်ယောက်နဲ့ အမှတ်မထင်ဆုံခဲ့တယ်။ ရုတ်တရက် သူ့လက်ထဲက ရောင်စုံဖလင်ပြားလေးတွေ ကျွန်တော့်ကို ကမ်းပြီးတစ်ကတ်ရွေးပြီးရင်အဲ့မင်းရွေးလိုက်တဲ့ကတ် က ဘာအရောင်ဆိုတာ အဲရွေးထားတဲ့ကတ်ပေါ်ပြန်ရေးပေး ။ မင်းမှန်မယ်ထင်လားဆိုပြီး ပြော လာတယ်။

ဘဝမှာ မေးခံရနေကြ ဖြေဖူးနေကြ မေးခွန်းမျိုးမဟုတ်ပေမယ့် ဒီတိုင်း မျက်လှည့်ပြတာဒါမှမဟုတ် တခုခုပဲဆိုတာမျိုး တွေးမိတာရယ် ဒီမေးခွန်းကို ဘာကြောင့်မှန်းမသိ ဖြေဖို့ လိုတယ်လို့ခံစားရတာလွဲရင် မဖြေခင် တခြား ထူးထူးခြားခြားဘာမှမရှိ။ ဒီလောက်လွယ်တာကိုလို့ စိတ်ထဲက တွေးပြီး သူ့ကိုတောင် ဘာမှရှည်ရှည်​ဝေးဝေးပြန် မပြော ထွေထွေထူးထူးမစဉ်းစား တော့ပဲ အဆင်ပြေရာ တစ်ကတ်ဆွဲယူဖို့ချက်ချင်းဟန် ပြင်လိုက်တယ်။ အဲဒီမှာတင်ကျွန်တော့်ကို တစ်ဖက်လူက တစ်ချက် ဟန့်တားလိုက်ပြီး ပြောတယ်

"အရင်ကဖြေသွားခဲ့တဲ့သူတွေရဲ့ အဖြေနဲ့ မင်းဖြေထားတာ အဖြေမတူရင် မှားရင် မင်းအတွက် မကောင်းတာတွေရှိသလို မှန်ရင်လဲမင်းအတွက်ကောင်းတာတွေဖြစ်လာမယ်တဲ့"

လွယ်ကူတဲ့အရာလို့ ထင်ထားတဲ့မေးခွန်းဆိုသည့်တိုင် ရုတ်တရက် နဲနဲတော့ တွန့်သွားတယ်။ အဲ့တာနဲ့ အဝါရောင်လို့ခံစားရတဲ့ ကတ်လေးကို သေချာရွေးယူလိုက်တယ်၊၊ ဒါပေမယ့် အရောင်မရေး​ခင် သေချာပြန်ကြည့်တော့ လိမ္မော်ရောင်လို့စိတ်ထဲ ထင်လာတယ်။ အဲ့တာနဲ့ အနီရောင်လို့ထင်ရတဲ့ကတ်လေးကိုပါယူပြီး အဝါရောင်ကတ်လေးနဲ့ထပ်ကြည့်တော့ မီးခိုးရောင်ရင့်ရင့်ထွက်လာတယ်။ ကတ်ပြားလေးတွေအကုန်တစ်ကတ်စီယူကြည့်လိုက် နှစ်ကတ်သုံးကတ် ထပ်ကြည့်လိုက်နဲ့ တော်တော်ကြာသွားတယ်။ တစ်ကတ်ထဲရွေးပြီး အရောင်ရေးပေးရမယ့် testဟာလွယ်လွယ်ပြီးရမယ့်အစား ချွေးသာပျံလာတယ်။ ဘယ်ကတ်ကဘာရောင်ဆိုတာမသဲကွဲတော့။ လက်ထဲက ကတ်တွေကိုငိုင်ကြည့်ရင်း စဉ်းစားနေတာဘယ်လောက်ကြာသွားတယ်မသိ ရုတ်တရက် ချောင်းဟန့်သံကြားမှ သတိပြန်ဝင်လာတယ်။

ကတ်ရွေးခိုင်းတဲ့သူက ဘယ်လိုလဲ ခက်နေပြီလားဆိုတဲ့ခနဲ့တဲ့ ပုံနဲ့ ကျွန်တော့်ကို တစ်ချက်ရယ်ပြတယ်။ အဲ့ဒါနဲ့ကျွန်တော်လဲ တွေ့ရာကတ်လေးတစ်ကတ်ကိုဆွဲယူပြီး ရွေးလိုက်တဲ့ကတ်ကို တစ်ဖက်ကလူ မြင်အောင်ပြလိုက်တယ်။ ပြီးတော့ အဲဒီရွေးလိုက်တဲ့ ဖလင်ကတ်ပြားလေးရဲ့တစ်ဖက်မှာအဖြူရောင်လို့ရေးပြီး နောက်ကျောဘက်ကို တော့အမဲရောင်လို့ရေးလိုက်တယ်၊၊ ရေးပြီးတာနဲ့ ကတ်လေးတွေကို ပိုင်ရှင်ဆီ ပြန်ပေးပြီး အဲဒီလူကိုစကားတစ်ခွန်းမှမပြောပဲ ကျောခိုင်း ခဲ့တယ်။ ခြေ လေးငါးဆယ်လှမ်း အကွာလောက်အရောက်ကျတော့ နောက်ကနေ လှမ်းအော်ပြောတဲ့ အဲဒီလူရဲ့

"ကောင်လေး မင်းကိုငါပေးလိုက်တာ ကတ်တွေကသာများနေတာ color fundamental အရ အခြေခံဖြစ်တဲ့ ဝါစိမ်းနီ သုံးရောင်ထဲဟ" ဆိုတဲ့အသံကို နားထဲ သေသေချာချာကြားလိုက်ရတယ်။ဒါပေမယ် ကျွန်တော့်ခေါင်းထဲမှာ တော့ ယူခဲ့တဲ့ကတ်နဲ့ ရေးခဲ့တဲ့အရောင် တူမတူ၊မှန်မမှန်၊ မှားမမှား ဆိုတဲ့ အတွေးတွေစိုးရိမ်မှုတွေ မရှိ။အဖြူနဲ့အမဲ
